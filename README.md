1. The Fracture in Modern Physics
For more than a century, modern physics has operated as a house divided. On the macro scale,
Einstein’s General Relativity models the universe as a smooth, continuous geometric fabric in
which gravity is the bending of space and time. On the micro scale, quantum mechanics rules
the subatomic world through a discrete, probabilistic field of particles. Whenever these two
frameworks are forced together at cosmic boundary points — the centre of a black hole, the first
instant of the Big Bang — the calculations collapse into meaningless infinities.
Conventional unification attempts, most notably string theory, have tried to repair this fracture
with abstract multi-dimensional mathematics. Yet by confining themselves to subatomic
interactions, TQTU’s proponents argue, they fail the true mandate of a Theory of Everything. A
genuine theory of nature, the framework insists, cannot stop at the particle accelerator. It must
scale without a break — from quantum gravity to human consciousness, from galactic structure
to the global economy.
